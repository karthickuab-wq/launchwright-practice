# Lessons

What this project learned, from each checkpoint's retrospective and from anything that went wrong.

## Lessons
Each lesson: what happened, the whole kind of problem it belongs to, and the rule that stops it happening again. Lessons that apply to every project are passed on to the plugin.

### Nov 23, 2026 · A quick market check called a field open when it wasn't
- **What happened:** the quick check for job photo reports looked only at Google Play listings and web search results. Its verdict said no simple, cheap app for solo tradespeople existed. A second review (Muse, shared by the user) found about a dozen live rivals. Reading their own websites through the built-in browser confirmed several: JobPics ("The photo app for solo service pros", iOS and Android), Logged ($9.99 a month, solo contractors, before/after photos on invoices), SiteCam (free tier: 500 photos, 25 reports), Jobshot ($29 a month per company). CompanyCam's Core plan is now sold "For Solo & Small Teams". The business plan's key claim was wrong.
- **The kind of problem:** saying "nobody does this" from sources that can't see the whole shelf. App Store pages are never read, and Google Play search is off limits, so many iPhone-first and new apps are invisible to a Play-only check.
- **The rule:** a market check never says a gap exists ("no app found", "open", "nobody does this") until it has also searched for the job's names plus "app" on the open web, and read the makers' own websites of the iPhone-only apps that search finds. The cloud often can't reach those sites, so use the built-in browser on the user's computer when it is connected. Count apps found that way in the competition verdict, even when their ratings can't be read. A low download count among rivals is a warning about demand, not proof of a gap. (For the kit's market-check skill: suggest adding this to its section 5.)
